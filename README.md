# Slipstream Labs

Slipstream Labs Website Brief

Company

Slipstream Labs is a product discovery and strategy consultancy run by Sebastian Bouvier in Stockholm.

We help product teams work out what is worth building, why, and what the evidence says before they commit significant time and money.

The company works primarily with post-seed and Series A teams that have a product idea, problem, or strategic uncertainty but need clearer direction before investing heavily in development.

Website goal

The website should:

Clearly explain what Slipstream Labs does within a few seconds.

Establish credibility and trust without feeling like a traditional consultancy.

Make the offering feel concrete and outcome-oriented.

Encourage potential clients to get in touch.

Feel like a modern product company rather than an agency.

The site should be minimal, sharp, confident, and highly intentional.

Positioning

The core idea is:

Work out what is worth building before you build it.

Slipstream Labs helps teams move from uncertainty to evidence-backed direction through structured product discovery.

The emphasis should be on:

Evidence over assumptions

Clarity over complexity

Learning before building

Decisions over deliverables

Fast, focused discovery

Avoid generic consultancy language such as "unlock value", "drive transformation", "holistic solutions", etc.

Target audience

Primarily:

Founders

Product leaders

Product managers

Early-stage leadership teams

Especially teams that are asking questions such as:

Are we solving the right problem?

Do customers actually need this?

Which opportunity should we pursue?

What should we build first?

Are we ready to invest in development?

How do we reduce the risk before committing engineering resources?

Core offering

The main offering is a Discovery Sprint.

A fixed-scope engagement designed to rapidly investigate a product opportunity and provide a clear recommendation on what to do next.

Typical activities can include:

Problem framing

Customer/user research

Market and competitor analysis

Opportunity assessment

Hypothesis testing

Prototyping

Experiment design

Evidence synthesis

Product strategy

Prioritisation

The output should not be presented as a pile of documents.

The outcome is clarity and a decision.

For example:

Build it. Change it. Test it. Or don't build it.

Suggested website structure

1. Hero

Very clear headline focused on the core proposition.

Something in the direction of:

Work out what is worth building.

Supporting copy explaining that Slipstream Labs helps product teams validate opportunities and make better product decisions before committing to development.

Primary CTA:

Start a conversation

Secondary CTA could be:

See how it works

2. The problem

Explain the cost of building the wrong thing.

Teams often move from an idea directly into design and development without sufficiently understanding:

The problem

The customer

The opportunity

The assumptions

Whether the solution is actually worth building

Slipstream Labs exists to create a deliberate step between idea and build.

3. The approach

Show a simple, visual process rather than a complicated consulting framework.

For example:

Frame → Investigate → Test → Decide

Each step should be concise.

4. Discovery Sprint

Present the Discovery Sprint as the primary productised service.

Clearly communicate:

What it is

When to use it

What happens during the sprint

What the client gets

What decision it enables

Make it feel like something a product team can actually buy, rather than an abstract consulting engagement.

5. Outcomes

Focus on decisions rather than deliverables.

Examples:

Know the problem
Understand whether you are solving something that actually matters.

Understand the opportunity
Build an evidence-based view of the market, users, and potential.

Test your assumptions
Find out what holds up before committing to development.

Know what to do next
Leave with a clear direction and the evidence behind it.

6. About

Introduce Sebastian briefly.

Position him as an experienced product professional with a background spanning product management, business analysis, and data across multiple industries.

The tone should be personal and credible, not a traditional consultant biography.

7. CTA

End with a simple invitation:

Have a product decision you're stuck on?

Let's work out what is worth building.

Start a conversation

Visual direction

The visual identity should feel closer to Linear, Stripe, or a high-quality modern product company than a traditional consultancy.

Characteristics:

Minimal

Lots of whitespace

Strong typography

Very restrained colour palette

Precise spacing

Subtle borders

Subtle interaction and motion

No generic stock photography

No corporate handshake imagery

No excessive gradients

No unnecessary illustrations

Use a primarily white / off-white / dark grey palette with a restrained accent colour if needed.

Typography should feel modern and highly legible, with a strong sans-serif similar in character to Linear's typography.

The design should feel quietly confident rather than flashy.

Important design principle

Avoid the typical consultancy website structure where every section is a large card, rounded box, or decorative graphic.

The page should feel more like a well-designed product interface.

Use typography, spacing, hierarchy, lines, grids, and subtle interaction to create structure.

The visual system should communicate:

Clarity. Precision. Evidence. Momentum.

Technical

Build the site as a fast, responsive modern website.

Prioritise:

Desktop and mobile responsiveness

Excellent typography

Fast loading

Clean semantic HTML

SEO basics

Accessible contrast and navigation

Subtle animations rather than heavy effects

Easy future editing of copy and sections

The final result should feel like a premium product discovery practice, not a freelance portfolio or generic consulting agency.
